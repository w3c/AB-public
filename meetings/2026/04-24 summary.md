## 2026-04-24 Advisory Board F2F meeting Day 2

What follows is a brief summary of the topics discussed on Day 2 of the W3C Advisory Board F2F meeting on 2026-04-24

attending: Daniel Appelquist, Hidde de Vries, Wei Ding, Emma Fraser, Max Gendler, Tatsuya Igarashi, Elena Lape, An Qi (Angel) Li, Theresa O'Conner,  Hiroshi Ota, Avneesh Singh, Amy van der Hiel, Song Xu, Brent Zundel


* **Process Refactoring 1**
The AB discussed the AB breakout and guidance for the Process CG. Brent noted there were two main topics covered: tooling and adjustments to WGs. The group discussed requests for minutes taking, auto-scribing, what the Process requires for permission for recording, needs of different groups, and what nuances are required for how groups operate. The AB discussed auto-transcription for minute taking and Brent suggested inviting proponents of different systems and those who use them to have conversations and the AB felt this was valuable. However, it was noted that use of recording could mean some people don't participate in certain groups. Brent asked for people willing to drive the auto-transcription topic and Hidde offered.

The AB also discussed use of AI in horizontal review giving an example of someone using it in TAG reviews. It was noted to be careful to distinguish horizontal from wide review. There was a feeling that automating tooling for some parts of the review process could be beneficial though there were concerns about whether the results would be trustworthy and that having humans in the loop to verify all information would be necessary. 

The group discussed more lightweight chartering while maintaining a Process with needed reality checks, deliverables and scope. The AB noted some deliverables or change of scope might be ok if done with group consensus while others might need AC review. The AB discussed timelines and whether they were not needed or whether they were an important forcing function, a measure of group heartbeat or even an important signal for funding by employers, government policies, other SDOs or even external dependencies. The AB discussed that the Process was often seen as being complicated and long and that it might be possible to split up the document into standards sections and then everything else (eg: elections).  Brent noted that a goal of refactoring is to improve the user experience of using the Process. He noted that feedback has included "using the process is difficult in x way" so the way to proceed is not to start out by trying to make a less complex document, but rather, to focus on making a less difficult process. 

* **Strategic Initiatives**
The AB discussed the Strategic Objectives. The group noted it did not know of updates about the Technical Strategy objective and would like to understand what has been happening and would reach out to PLH for an update. The group discussed the Policy TF and noted they would request to Tara that Hidde be added to that TF. Ota-san gave an update of the work of the Structural Evolution TF which had been developing frameworks for stakeholders and draft outreach though the AB did feel the work should now be moving to actions. Avneesh gave an update on the Impact Framework TF. He noted DingWei would be joining and feedback on the documents was welcome. The AB suggested the TFs might coordinate monthly. The AB discussed the Structural Evolution TF presentation by Ian on OSS. While they supported the idea of more involvement in OSS they did feel that should not be the only focus of the TF. AB members who are on the TF mentioned they were excited to join work about the evolution of the W3C and did emphasize that they felt the focus should be on the whole evolution of the Consortium. They felt the OS idea should be a side project. Dan took the action to talk to Ian. Team confirmed they would invite Seth to a future meeting to get more detailed updates on the TFs and future plans. The AB also discussed the work of the Membership TF and concerns about member attrition. 


* **Process Refactoring 2**
The AB discussed evolution of the AC as part of the evolution of the Process and the potential for the AC to be a more deliberative and a more effective body. However, it was also noted that the AB and TAG could be considered to have been delegated by AC to deliberate. AB passed on AC feedback that AC reps didn't want to engage on ac-forum and so maybe it would be better to try to engage AC on the work W3C wants to do. There were some concerns about the level of attendance at the AC meeting. Amy gave some metrics on the AC list and voting, participation in groups etc.  She noted that participation on ac-forum is very low and most work happens in Groups and on GH. The AB discussed negative perception of behavior on mailing lists but also noted that people who attend TPAC tend to be energized and see W3C at its best. An example was shared of a new person at the AC having very positive interactions. The AB mentioned the Engagement Group proposal as one positive potential solution. It was noted that the AC rep is the point of entry for members so effort should be put in to ensure they recognize the value of W3C and that if an AC rep feels ownership of an organization they will be more committed to it. The AB reiterated that focusing on technical topics and the work of the Consortium was important. It was encouraged that AC meetings be designed to get AC input instead of just sharing updates. The AB touched on the AB election where candidates were sat uncontested and ways to increase the impact of the Monthly Member Meetings. The AB also discussed holding a meetup in Rotterdam to talk to the local community. 


* **Potential Position Papers**
The AB discussed potential  position papers including: on regulatory engagement in areas like the Open Web, Security or the impact of LLM crawlers (though noting that the remit of the AB is not technical but advising); of the role of ABs around the world; lessons from the landscape of in person, telecoms; about AI and accessibility; and why involving new folks including students and young people, in standards is important. 


* **Chatham House Retro**
The AB held a Chatham House Rules style (comments not assigned to a person) style retro of the AC meeting mentioning "roses" (positives, good things), "thorns" (something that could have gone better or a worry), and "buds" (something they are excited about).  

Roses (good things) included: positive discussions with Team, many attendees went to the mic, international dialogues, different ideas and perspectives, the meetup, the meeting was very well managed, the AB being reasonable and collectively working toward the same goal, trying new formats at the meeting, feeling included, the opportunity to engage Asian members more, bold and open-minded engagements, opportunities for remote attendees to talk together, implementation of the feedback given at the previous AC, amazing live translation, and people crossing geo-political boundaries. 

Buds (exciting things) included: dinner with the Board, that the AB panel was full on stage, the next TPAC,  AC meetings moving forward, engaging with local communities, outreach, location of TPAC 2027 in Panama and engagement in the global South, the enthusiasm people had for the impact of the web, and more 1:1 communication with the AC.

Thorns (problems or worries) included: talks could have been condensed down,  automatic translation was disabled on zoom, would have liked more presentations from the AC rather than to them, reading material was not sent in advance of travel, that CEO and other officers didn't attend in person, low attendance, there could have been greater sensitivity to women and LGBTQ people, and competing breakouts.
