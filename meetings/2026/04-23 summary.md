## 2026-04-23 Advisory Board F2F meeting Day 1

What follows is a brief summary of the topics discussed on Day 1 of the W3C Advisory Board F2F meeting on 2026-04-23

attending: Daniel Appelquist, Hidde de Vries, Wei Ding, Emma Fraser, Tatsuya Igarashi, An Qi (Angel) Li, Theresa O'Conner,  Hiroshi Ota, Avneesh Singh, Amy van der Hiel, Song Xu, Brent Zundel


* **Feedback from the AC Meeting**
AB members gave feedback on the AC meeting. Feedback was especially positive about the opportunities to engage with the local community in China, simultaneous translation, and the breakout on cultural inclusivity. The group also discussed feedback about the Process CG updates, feedback from AC reps on what they would like to hear about, meeting materials, TF participation and some concerns about attendance and connectivity. 

* **AC Rep MC Rep : Review of Poll Feedback, next steps**
The AB discussed responses to the Advisory Committee and Consortium Member Rep poll. Results seemed to indicate there was confusion on the division of the roles. Some AB felt that while there's some difference in theory, in practice the difference may not need to be big. The group also discussed the issue of reaching quorum in Consortium Member Rep meetings and communicating the importance of the meetings. It was resolved that Tess would draft a consensus of the AB to share with Members. 

* **Incubation of New Work**
DingWei mentioned the presentation by Heather Flanagan in February on the DISPATCH process at IETF and proposed something similar might be done at W3C. He also mentioned liaisons and the idea of W3C Ambassadors. The AB discussed the possibilities of and logistics that would be needed to do a DISPATCH-like program at TPAC and suggested one main session where all attendees would attend. They noted the different cultures in W3C and IETF and the different structures for new work. The AB requested DingWei to take the feedback back to the Exploration IG to develop a specific proposal. 

On the topic of liaising with other organizaitons the AB  discussed inviting Members involved in other SDOs to participate in "briefings from peer organizations" for information exchange at the Monthly Member Meetings. There was discussion whether an official role ("ambassador", "liaison" or "explorer") might be an incentive or whether it was better to avoid titles. The AB was supportive of understanding who in the W3C is involved in other SDOs and suggested creating and sharing a wiki page so other members could add themselves to form a voluntary registry, though not to attach an official title. 

* **Upcoming Election**
The AB discussed the upcoming AB election, "Meet the Candidate" sessions, and what steps the last AB followed for collecting candidate questions. Hidde offered to review past questions and open a wiki and Issue for collecting new questions. 

* **Advisory Committee/Consortium Member Rep retro**
The AB did a retro on the recent Advisory Committee/Consortium Member Rep poll. Amy gave an overview of the timeline and the results so far. She noted that this was a new process and so the process was still being worked out, some information was not available or hard to access, and that the topic was about governance which can be contentious. One misunderstanding that was clarified was that while the issue was proposed to be put to a poll, not everyone intended that the new ac-poll system be the mechanism employed and this might have increased the confusion/cycles involved. The AB noted the point of AC polls was for AC to engage with each other though they did note that there had been no other polls since they were launched in January.

* **Position Statements**
The AB discussed input Hidde and Elena have received since the AI in Web Standards position statement was published. They noted they'd been tracking feedback and the AB discussed if and how updates might be made. The AB resolved that AB position statements represent AB consensus opinion at a point in time and that for the AI position paper, they would not make incremental updates but might potentially make a larger update in the future in which they may incorporate community feedback.

The AB reviewed Dan's draft of a position statement on "Adopting a Human Rights Posture at W3C" which he had adjusted based on previous feedback. The AB was in favor of the direction of the work and discussed suggestions including increasing engagement with civil society groups as well as monitoring and an ethical review process. The AB did note that while there was Team input, Team involvement would be vital and suggested to distinguish what parts of the paper are the statement and which are suggested activities.
